## The Case for Design-Build in the Mountains

Most renovation projects follow the same sequence: design first, price later. An architect or designer draws plans. The homeowner approves them. Then a contractor bids the work. The problem is that the contractor isn't in the room while cost-driving decisions are being made. By the time the bid comes back, the homeowner is emotionally committed to a design that may be significantly over what they actually wanted to spend.

Design-build closes that gap. When the contractor is part of the design process from the start, every decision gets made with a real number attached to it. You know what a layout change costs before you decide to make it. The scope holds because someone is accountable for holding it from day one. The design that gets built is one that was realistic before it was ever drawn.

One team, one conversation at every phase, from initial meeting to final walkthrough.
